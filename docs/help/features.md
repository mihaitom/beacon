# Features

**Casting is the point.** Beacon grew around a backend that talks to real speakers on its own, and the rest of the app is built on top of that. Everything you would expect is here too - a full library (albums, artists, tracks, genres, playlists, favorites, with local filtering and search), a home page built from your own listening, top tracks, artists, albums and formats on a Stats page, multiselect and bulk queue/playlist actions, starring and rating, synced or unsynced lyrics, light and dark themes or one of your own, and a UI in German, English, Spanish, French and Italian. What makes Beacon worth running is below.

- **Cast to Sonos, AirPlay, Chromecast and DLNA - and to several at once.** Take over a device someone else is using (with a confirmation prompt), set each device's volume independently, and let AirPlay 2 pairing handle the devices that require it (HomePods, Apple TVs). The queue advances server-side, so playback keeps going even when the controlling window is asleep or a phone's screen is locked, and lyrics and the visualizer follow each device's own real position instead of a fixed guess. Local and cast share one session, one queue and one clock rather than being two loosely joined halves.
- **Gapless playback, and crossfades the way a DJ mixes.** Songs follow each other without a pause, or blend into each other: where both have a steady beat and their tempos are close enough, Beacon eases the outgoing song into the next one's tempo, lines up their downbeats and swaps the basses on one; otherwise it is an ordinary crossfade. Each kind has a length of its own, and songs that follow each other on an album always play gaplessly. The mix is made on the Beacon server as one continuous stream, so it sounds the same in the app, on every kind of speaker and for online party guests, and Now Playing announces the next song as the transition begins. Under Settings, Playback, Transitions.
- **Multi-user** - different logins on the same deployment each get independent playback to independent devices at the same time.
- **Remote Control and the mobile web UI** - pair a phone to the already-running desktop app over the LAN by QR code or PIN, no app install, and drive local playback from it (Queue, Library with artist pages, Favorites, Playlists, Radio, and Now Playing from the player bar), in your phone's own language. On the Docker/web build there is nothing to pair: the responsive mobile UI _is_ the player and keeps going through a screen lock. See [Remote Control and the mobile web UI](#remote-control-electron-and-the-mobile-web-ui).
- **Party mode** - the party button hands guests a QR code or a link and nothing else: they see what is coming up, search the library, wish for songs, withdraw their own wishes and vote to skip - no pausing, no speakers, no volume. Everything guests use lives under `/party/`, so a reverse proxy can let just that past its login. See [Party mode](#party-mode).
- **Online party** - for a group that meets over a voice chat instead of in one room, everyone hears the music in their own browser from the party link, at the quality you pick (AAC 128, 192 or 256 kbit/s), with wishes, skip votes and lyrics and visualizer following what each listener hears. The host casts or plays locally as always. Needs Beacon reachable from the internet. See [Online party](#online-party).
- **Internet radio, and a way to find it.** Add and manage your own stations, or use **Discover stations** to search the free [Radio Browser](https://www.radio-browser.info/) directory by name, filter by country (remembered for next time), and sort by most-played (the default) or most-voted - trying any result straight away before saving it. Stations that broadcast a "now playing" tag show the current artist and track wherever you look, and keep a time-stamped running list of what they have played, kept on the Beacon server so it carries on filling while you cast with the app closed.
- **Discovery that leans on what you actually play.** Home offers a Rediscover shelf of songs you played a lot but have not heard in months, a Discover shelf seeded from artists you really listen to (via MusicBrainz + ListenBrainz) or from your own ListenBrainz history, and a "New to explore" shelf of similar artists not yet in your library. **Song and Artist Radio** and **Autoplay** top a queue up with similar tracks before it runs dry, and a **playlist builder** fills a new playlist from Last.fm (country or genre charts, an artist's top tracks, tracks similar to one of yours, your own most played) or ListenBrainz, using only the tracks your library actually has.
- **Preview an album or playlist** - about two minutes from six songs spread across it, different ones each time, the loudest part of each blended into the next, while your queue stays as it was. "Play from here" plays it from the song you are hearing. On album and playlist pages and in the list of playlists, in the app and on speakers.
- **Smart playlists for Navidrome** - build one from rules (genre, year, rating, how often and how recently you played it, and more) on a page of its own, with a preview from your library that answers while you write them, or start from one of a handful of ideas. Rules Beacon cannot edit, from an `.nsp` file or another client, are kept as they are. On the desktop layout; see [the FAQ](faq/library.md#why-does-beacon-ask-for-my-navidrome-password-to-edit-a-smart-playlist) for why it asks for your Navidrome password.
- **Adjustable audio quality - a ceiling, not a bitrate.** Anything already under your limit streams untouched; only what is above it gets converted down as it plays. Set separately for this device's own playback and for casting, per device.
- **Home Assistant** - the desktop app finds its own way onto your network over mDNS, so the integration needs no configuration: now playing with cover, playback and volume, picking the speakers, and your playlists, albums and radio stations in Home Assistant's media browser, all usable in automations. A ready-made dashboard turns up in the sidebar. See [home-assistant/](../../home-assistant/).
- **A Now Playing screen worth looking at** - fullscreen, with a real-time frequency visualizer and ReplayGain (track or album gain) over a background of artist photos. On phones and tablets those make way for playback that survives a screen lock - see [the FAQ](faq/playback.md#why-is-there-no-visualizer-replaygain-or-volume-slider-in-the-mobile-web-player).
- **Artist images from Fanart.tv** - a background photo on the artist and album pages and behind Now Playing, the artist's logo in place of the plain name, and banners across the library headers. Works without setup, can be switched off under Library in Settings, and the images are cached on the Beacon server.
- **A Privacy page that names every outside service Beacon contacts** - what each one is asked for, what is sent with the request, and whether the connection comes from your device or your Beacon server. Anything a setting can switch off says which setting that is, and the lyrics providers can be turned off one by one or all at once, so a song's title and artist never leave your server.

## Remote Control (Electron) and the mobile web UI

Two different answers to "control Beacon from my phone", depending on how you run it:

- **Electron (desktop app):** Settings → Remote Control pairs a phone to _that specific, already-running_ desktop window over the LAN - scan a QR code or type in a short PIN, no app install on the phone. It relays commands to the same local playback the desktop window already has open (Queue, Library, Favorites, Playlists, Radio, and Now Playing from the player bar), not to casting sessions, which already have their own independent per-device control. The pairing credential is a long random token separate from `CONNECT_TOKEN`, regenerated every time the feature is (re-)enabled, and the feature auto-disables if the desktop app goes away.
- **Home automation (Electron):** Settings → Advanced → Home automation creates a key that lets Home Assistant or another tool use the same interface without pairing, and keeps working across restarts. While a key exists the app announces itself over mDNS; a fixed port can be set there too. A Home Assistant integration and dashboard are in [home-assistant/](../../home-assistant/); the interface itself is described in [the home automation guide](guides/home-automation.md).
- **Docker/web:** there's no separate desktop window to pair against - the browser tab already is the player. Opening the deployment directly on a phone's browser switches to a responsive mobile layout automatically; nothing to enable.

## Party mode

The party button in the player bar invites guests with a QR code or a link: they get a page in Beacon's look (on a computer Now Playing with artist background and synced lyrics, on a phone the mobile layout, the visualizer while casting), see what is coming up, search the library and wish for songs, which take turns between guests right after the current one. They can withdraw their own wishes and vote to skip, and nothing else - no pausing, no speakers, no volume. Works in the desktop app and the Docker/web build; everything guests use lives under `/party/`, so a reverse proxy can let just that past its login. See [the party mode guide](guides/party-mode.md) for the security model and examples for Traefik, Nginx Proxy Manager and Caddy.

## Online party

For a group that meets online instead of in one room - friends in a voice chat, a stream, a remote team's call - with the host bringing the music. Switch on "Online party" in the party dialog and everyone gets the music in their own browser from the party link, at the quality you pick (AAC 128, 192 or 256 kbit/s), together with the rest of party mode: what's coming up, wishes, skip votes, and lyrics and visualizer following what each of them hears. Nothing changes for the host: cast or play locally as always, radio included. Guests listen a few seconds behind the host. It needs Beacon reachable from the internet behind a reverse proxy - see [Over the internet](guides/party-over-the-internet.md) for setups with Traefik, Nginx Proxy Manager, Caddy, Authentik and Authelia - so it is only offered in the Docker/web build, opened under its public address; the desktop app's party link only reaches the local network. Each listener costs about the chosen bitrate in upload. See [the party mode guide](guides/party-mode.md#online-party).

## Jellyfin and Plex support

Jellyfin and Plex can both be selected as a server type at login. Neither has a Subsonic-compatible API of its own, so the `connect` backend translates Subsonic-shaped requests into real Jellyfin/Plex API calls on the fly (see `connect/media/jellyfin_bridge.py` and `connect/media/plex_bridge.py`) - the frontend doesn't know the difference. Features a given backend has no equivalent for (or that just aren't bridged yet) are hidden automatically rather than shown as dead-end controls. Jellyfin is supported from **10.9** upwards, 12 included: those two generations differ in how a client authenticates and in the shape of the item endpoints, and Beacon speaks the form both of them accept. Both paths are newer and less exercised than the Navidrome/Subsonic one, and a couple of things genuinely work differently there - the table below says which. Besides the usual unit tests, which can only check the bridges against Beacon's own understanding of the foreign API, there is a suite that runs them against real servers (`connect/tests/test_bridges_live.py`, excluded from the default test run): playlist creation, reordering, renaming and track add/remove, favorites, the browsing response shapes, library scans and lyrics - against Navidrome as well, since the assumptions Beacon makes about it are just as worth checking. It creates only throwaway playlists and deletes them again. Cover art and audio streaming are covered too, including the bare stream URL that casting and local playback hand to FFmpeg, which authenticates differently from every other call.

| Feature                                               | Navidrome / Subsonic |        Jellyfin        |              Plex              |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | :------------------: | :--------------------: | :----------------------------: |
| Library browsing, playlists, casting                  |          ✅          |           ✅           |               ✅               |
| Internet radio stations                               |          ✅          |           ✅           |               ✅               |
| Play history / Stats page                             |          ✅          |           ✅           |               ✅               |
| Favorites (heart icon)                                |          ✅          |           ✅           |    ✅ (as a 5-star rating)     |
| Personal 1-5 star rating                              |          ✅          |           ❌           |               ✅               |
| Song/Artist Radio and Autoplay                        |          ✅          |           ✅           | ✅ (needs an active Plex Pass) |
| Create playlists, add/remove/reorder tracks           |          ✅          |           ✅           |               ✅               |
| Smart playlists recognised, their songs read-only     |          ✅          | ❌ (Jellyfin has none) |               ✅               |
| Create smart playlists and edit their rules (desktop) | ✅ (Navidrome only)  |           ❌           |               ❌               |
| Lyrics stored with the file (tags or .lrc)            |          ✅          |           ✅           |         ✅ (.lrc only)         |
| Trigger a library rescan from Settings                |     ✅ (admins)      |      ✅ (admins)       |           ✅ (owner)           |
| Limit casting to listed accounts (web/Docker)         |     ✅ (admins)      |      ✅ (admins)       |           ✅ (owner)           |

Song/Artist Radio and Autoplay all come from one call (`getSimilarSongs2.view`), and the three servers answer it very differently. Navidrome mixes the artist's own tracks in with similar ones. Jellyfin translates it to its InstantMix, whose quality stands or falls with the metadata: an artist carrying genre tags gets a genuinely matching mix (a Hip Hop artist returned 9 Hip Hop tracks out of 10 against a live library), while an artist without them gets something close to a random draw - and the seeded artist's own tracks never appear either way, so a Jellyfin radio is all discovery and no familiarity. If yours feels arbitrary, the artists are probably untagged; Jellyfin 12 also lets plugins supply similarity data, which is the other way to improve it. Plex needs an active Plex Pass here and returns nothing without one.

Lyrics are a two-step lookup everywhere: whatever is stored with the audio file itself comes first (it belongs to that exact recording), and only if there is none does Beacon search its own third-party providers - so lyrics work on all three, this row is only about the first step. Jellyfin serves the file's own lyrics to any signed-in user, from the tags or from an .lrc next to the track. Plex only reads the .lrc: a `USLT` tag that Navidrome and Jellyfin both pick up produces no lyrics there at all, verified against a live server. Where Plex finds nothing, the third-party lookup takes over exactly as it would for an untagged track.

Smart playlists fill themselves from rules, so on Navidrome and Plex Beacon labels them and offers no way to reorder, remove or add songs there. Creating one and editing its rules works on Navidrome alone: its rules live in Navidrome's own API rather than the Subsonic one, which no other Subsonic server shares and which Beacon reaches with your Navidrome password (see [the FAQ](faq/library.md#why-does-beacon-ask-for-my-navidrome-password-to-edit-a-smart-playlist)). Plex builds its smart playlists from filters of an entirely different kind, so those are made in Plex itself.

A library rescan is administrator-only on every server Beacon speaks to - Navidrome marks `startScan` as such in its own route table, Jellyfin's API requires elevation, and Plex's section refresh is owner-only. Beacon asks the server which kind of account is signed in (Subsonic's `getUser.view`, which the two bridges answer by translating it into Jellyfin's user policy and Plex's owner-only settings endpoint) and only offers the button to an administrator; a server that doesn't answer at all leaves it visible rather than hiding something that might have worked. What the button then reports differs: Navidrome counts the tracks it has processed, while Jellyfin and Plex only know a percentage, so that is what gets shown for them.

Song/Artist Radio and Autoplay on Plex are bridged onto Plex's own Sonic Analysis feature (`/library/metadata/{id}/nearest`, the same thing "Play Similar Tracks" uses in every official Plex client) - but Sonic Analysis itself is a Plex Pass-gated feature server-side, confirmed against Plex's own support docs. Without an active Plex Pass, the buttons and the Autoplay toggle are still there, they just quietly return nothing instead of a real mix. Plex's core Media Server API has no separate boolean favorite at all (the heart-shaped "Love" seen in Plexamp/mobile is backed by a different, Plex Pass cloud sync API this bridge doesn't talk to), so on Plex a favorite is a five-star rating, the same thing Plexamp's own "like" sets: marking a song as a favorite gives it five stars, and five stars put it in Favorites.

Casting can be controlled per account, so an administrator can share their Beacon without handing over the speakers: the policy is a whitelist or a blacklist, and a second switch decides what accounts not on the list - including ones that show up later - are allowed to do. Anyone who may not cast can still browse and listen locally. The policy is server-wide, kept per media server, the administrator is always allowed, and an untouched server lets everyone cast. It is offered in the Docker and web builds only - the desktop app runs a single-user backend of its own, where there is nobody to restrict. The picker offers the accounts that have signed in to this Beacon, plus Jellyfin's own full list. The account matched is the one the server reports for the signed-in credentials, not the name typed at login; on Plex, whose server cannot name its accounts, it is confirmed with plex.tv when someone signs in, so a Plex account that was already signed in before this Beacon version counts as not listed until it signs in again. See [docs/plans/cast-permissions.md](../plans/cast-permissions.md) for what this does and does not enforce.
